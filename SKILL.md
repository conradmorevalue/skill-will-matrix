# Skill/Will Matrix

## Core Principle

Stop wasting energy on the wrong people. Focus on the ones who WANT to grow.

Leaders with the strongest teams refuse to manage everyone the same way. They diagnose each person individually and adjust their approach. One of the simplest and most powerful tools is the Skill/Will Matrix (Max Landsberg). It plots every team member on two axes:

- **Skill** — ability and experience (can they do the job?)
- **Will** — motivation and drive (do they want to do the job and grow?)

The result is four distinct types of people, each requiring a very different leadership response. People rarely stay in one box forever — skill and will both shift over time. Lead each person according to where they are *right now*. That is where your energy compounds.

## The Four Quadrants

### 1. Top Performers (High Skill + High Will)
They deliver results, push themselves, and raise the bar for everyone.

**Leadership response:**
- Give ownership and autonomy
- Ask for their input on strategy and decisions
- Raise expectations and stretch them
- Recognize and celebrate often
- Invest in their growth (next-level roles, stretch projects)
- Avoid overloading or micromanaging — protect their energy so they can keep raising the bar

Build the future of the company around these people.

### 2. High Potentials (Low Skill + High Will)
Eager, motivated, and ready to grow — they just need opportunity and guidance.

**Leadership response:**
- Coach actively and frequently
- Break work into small, achievable wins
- Celebrate progress publicly
- Pair with strong mentors or Top Performers
- Provide clear learning paths and feedback
- Protect their enthusiasm; do not let early struggles kill momentum

These are your future Top Performers if you invest correctly.

### 3. Disengaged Experts (High Skill + Low Will)
Highly capable but unmotivated — often bored, burnt out, misaligned, or missing purpose.

**Leadership response:**
- Diagnose the disconnect (ask, don’t assume)
- Reconnect them to the mission or impact
- Adjust goals, responsibilities, or scope if needed
- Set a clear timeline for re-engagement
- If no improvement, exit respectfully — skilled people who stop caring drag standards down

Do not ignore the will problem just because the skill is high.

### 4. Low Performers (Low Skill + Low Will)
They drain energy and rarely improve without massive intervention.

**Leadership response:**
- Set crystal-clear expectations and a short improvement window
- Give one genuine chance with support
- Document the conversation
- Exit respectfully and quickly if no meaningful change
- Keeping them signals that standards are negotiable and demotivates everyone else

Protect the team’s energy and standards above individual comfort.

## How to Apply the Matrix

1. **Map the team** — Quietly place each direct report (or key person) on the matrix based on current evidence, not hope or history.
2. **Choose the leadership mode** — Match your time, attention, and style to the quadrant.
3. **Protect your energy** — Spend disproportionate time on Top Performers and High Potentials. Be decisive with the other two.
4. **Revisit regularly** — Skill and will change. A quarterly (or even monthly) recalibration conversation keeps the map accurate.
5. **Make it visible to yourself** — You do not need to share the labels with the team, but you must be honest with yourself about where people actually sit.

## Three Leadership Habits That Compound Results

1. **Notice what each person needs before deciding how to lead them.** When people feel understood, trust grows and progress becomes cultural.
2. **Protect your own energy by investing it where growth is welcomed.** Discernment creates boundaries and inspires the motivated.
3. **Revisit every team member’s progress regularly.** People change. Consistent conversations build confidence, accountability, and sustained success.

## Best Practices

- Never manage everyone the same way. Uniform treatment is the fastest way to waste energy and lose Top Performers.
- Will is often more important than skill in the long run. High-will people can acquire skill; low-will people rarely acquire drive.
- Exit decisions are leadership, not failure. Keeping Low Performers or Disengaged Experts who refuse to re-engage costs the whole system.
- Use the matrix as a thinking tool, not a permanent label. The goal is better leadership action today, not categorization forever.
- When in doubt, ask: “Where is this person on skill and will *right now*, and what does that require from me?”

## Diagnostic Questions

- Who on my team is a Top Performer that I am under-investing in?
- Who has high will but is stuck because I have not coached or stretched them enough?
- Who has high skill but low will — and have I diagnosed why?
- Who is draining energy with low skill *and* low will, and am I being decisive?
- Am I spending most of my leadership energy on the people who want to grow?
